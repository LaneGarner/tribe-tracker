User Roles & Permissions

How elevated permissions work for districts and schools (enterprise model)

Everyone in Tribe Tracker is fundamentally a user. Some users are simply granted more permissions — the ability to create challenges for a group and to see how their group is doing. There is no "admin" label shown anywhere in the app; these are real-world roles like District Wellness Manager and Building Manager. Behind the scenes, those users are flagged with a role that unlocks extra capabilities, scoped to the part of the organization they're responsible for.

1The hierarchy

Below the platform owner there are two elevated roles — one scoped to a whole district, one scoped to a single school/building — and then everyday staff who take part.

Platform Owner — you / Tribe Tracker
Sets up districts, manages billing & the overall app. Not a customer-facing role.
District Wellness ManagerElevated role 1
Scope: the entire district (all of its schools)
Sees who's using the app across the district and creates district-wide challenges.
Building ManagerElevated role 2
Scope: one school / building — typically a teacher or site wellness lead
Creates building challenges for that school's staff and sees their building's activity.
Staff / Participants
Scope: themselves
Join challenges, check in on habits, appear on leaderboards, chat.

Organizationally, a building lives inside a district, so a District Manager's view naturally includes the buildings beneath them. The two roles are "children" of the platform owner in the sense that the owner grants and oversees them.

3The key idea: challenges have an owner level

Permissions hang off scope. A challenge is created at a level, and that level decides who sees it.

District challenge

Created by the District Wellness Manager

  • Visible to staff at every school in the district
  • Good for district-wide initiatives (e.g. a step challenge for all employees)
  • Rolls up into district usage reporting
Building challenge

Created by a Building Manager (teacher)

  • Visible only to staff at that one school
  • Good for site-specific goals (e.g. one school's hydration challenge)
  • Stays contained to that building

4Example

District Wellness Manager creates a "District Step Challenge — January." Every staff member, at all 12 schools, can see and join it. She opens her dashboard and sees that 340 of 600 employees have signed up.

Teacher at Lincoln Elementary creates a "Lincoln Staff Hydration Challenge." Only Lincoln staff see it. The district manager can also see it (it's in her district); a teacher at a different school cannot.

A staff member opens the app, sees both challenges they're eligible for, joins, and checks in each day — exactly like any other Tribe Tracker user.

5How this maps to data

The app already has the scaffolding for this — districts and buildings tables and District / Building / Staff management screens. The model below builds on that.

  • User gains a role (staff · building_manager · district_manager) plus a district_id and, for building managers, a building_id. That pairing is what scopes their powers.
  • Challenge gains an owner_level (district or building) and the matching district_id / building_id. Visibility is "show me challenges for my building, plus district challenges for my district."
  • Permission checks are simple: can this user create at this level, and is the target within their district/building? No new "admin account" type is needed — it's a flag on the normal user.

6Notes

  • No "admin" in the UI. Internally it's a role flag; to users it reads as "District Wellness Manager" or "Building Manager" (or whatever official titles the district uses).
  • Monetization fit. This is a natural B2B unit: sell per district (or per seat), with the district manager as the buyer/owner of their organization's space.
  • Extensible. The same scope pattern supports future levels (e.g. region above district) without rethinking the model.
